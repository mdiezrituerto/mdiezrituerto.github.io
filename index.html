About

I am an applied economist working on questions at the intersection of labour economics and public policy.

My research focuses on labour-market decisions, gender, competition, and the allocation of opportunities in high-skilled settings. My work combines empirical analysis with administrative and other real-world data to study how institutions and incentives shape individual choices and labour-market outcomes.

Alongside my academic research, I work in the National Office of Foresight & Strategy at the Presidency of the Government of Spain, where I contribute to long-term policy analysis and strategic foresight. This has involved working on issues including migration and demographic change, artificial intelligence, and the future of the welfare state.

I am interested in research questions that emerge from real-world economic and policy challenges, and in how empirical evidence can contribute to better understanding and decision-making.

Research

Labour economics / Gender / Competition / Decision-making

I am an applied economist working primarily on labour economics, gender, competition, and decision-making.

My research examines how people make decisions in high-stakes professional environments and how competition and institutional settings affect access to opportunities.

My PhD thesis, Selection, Competition, and Allocation in High-Skilled Labour Markets: Evidence from Medical Training in Spain , studies the role of competition and selection in access to medical training and subsequent career choices.

Supervisors: Nagore Iriberri and Pedro Rey-Biel.
