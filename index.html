Applied economics · Research · Public policy

I work at the intersection of applied economic research and public policy, using evidence to understand how institutions, incentives, and economic change shape people’s opportunities and wellbeing.

My research examines labour markets, gender, competition, and decision-making. Alongside my academic work, I work on long-term policy questions at the National Office of Foresight & Strategy at the Presidency of the Government of Spain.

My work moves in both directions: from research to policy, and from policy challenges back to research questions.

Research & policy

I approach economic questions from two complementary perspectives: empirical research on individual behaviour and institutions, and direct experience with the policy questions that arise in practice.

Research

My academic research studies labour-market decisions, gender, competition, and the allocation of opportunities in high-skilled settings. My PhD thesis examines how selection and competition shape access to medical training and subsequent career choices in Spain.

Policy

In government, I work on long-term questions where economic evidence must be considered alongside uncertainty, institutional constraints, and competing policy objectives. My work has included migration and demographic change, artificial intelligence, and the future of the welfare state.

From policy questions to research questions

Real-world policy challenges often raise questions that cannot be answered by existing evidence alone. Economic research can help identify mechanisms, test explanations, and understand how institutions and incentives shape outcomes.

This is the perspective I bring to applied research: starting from questions that matter, using rigorous empirical methods to understand them, and keeping sight of the institutions and people to whom the evidence ultimately matters.

Research

Labour economics / Gender / Behavioural economics

My research focuses on how people make important educational and career decisions, and how competition, incentives, and institutional environments affect access to opportunities.

My PhD thesis, Selection, Competition, and Allocation in High-Skilled Labour Markets: Evidence from Medical Training in Spain , studies these questions in the context of medical training. Supervised by Nagore Iriberri and Pedro Rey-Biel.

Policy work

My policy work focuses on long-term economic and social change, particularly where evidence needs to be combined with uncertainty about future developments.

  • España ante el reto migratorio foresight study

  • Foresight capacity-building across ministries training

  • Climate adaptation economics consultancy · Ricardo E&E
